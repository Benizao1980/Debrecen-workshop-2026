# Instructor answer key and discussion notes

## Focal isolate 1 — PubMLST 46556

From the slide-deck record:

- **PubMLST ID:** 46556
- **isolate:** P3-2209
- **country:** Japan
- **year:** 2010
- **source:** wild bird
- **comments:** Pigeon (Unknown dove)
- **species:** *Campylobacter jejuni*
- **ST:** 2209
- **clonal complex:** ST-179 complex

The related records shown in the deck include multiple Japanese pigeon/wild-bird isolates with ST2209/CC179. This is the teaching example for a lineage with strong bird-host structure.

### Interpretation

Good answer:
> “The available PubMLST context strongly supports a bird-associated population, but the isolate record alone does not identify an individual transmission route or direction.”

Avoid:
> “It came from pigeons.”

That overstates what a host-associated population pattern can establish.

## Focal isolate 2 — PubMLST 46122

From the slide-deck record:

- **PubMLST ID:** 46122
- **isolate:** KAI44/12
- **country:** Finland
- **year:** 2012
- **source:** goose
- **comments:** Goose (Unknown goose)
- **species:** *Campylobacter jejuni*
- **ST:** 45
- **clonal complex:** ST-45 complex

The broader CC45 context shown in the slides contains **250 isolates** and multiple source categories, including human and chicken isolates. Most of the displayed population is ST45, demonstrating that a common MLST label can encompass a broad ecological population.

### Interpretation

Good answer:
> “The sample was obtained from a goose, but CC45 is a host/source generalist. The genome therefore carries weaker source-specific signal than a host-associated lineage such as CC179.”

Key distinction:
- **sample host:** goose;
- **ecological population:** generalist CC45;
- **source-attribution claim:** uncertain without a properly sampled comparative model;
- **direct transmission:** not established.

## Exercise 1 — dataset dashboard

Participants should find the **Mourkas et al. 2024 Current Biology** publication filter and a dashboard of **700 genomes**.

The collection is geographically heterogeneous. The correct interpretation is “composition of this study collection,” not “global prevalence.”

## Exercise 2 — source × comments

Expected insight:
- broad source categories such as `wild bird`, `duck`, `goose`, `starling` can coexist with more specific species-level detail in `comments`;
- ecological analysis can be distorted if fine-scale host information is discarded;
- metadata harmonisation matters as much as genome resolution for many questions.

---

## Exercise 3 — MLST

- seven loci provide a stable, portable nomenclature;
- ST/CC are useful for rapid population context;
- they are too coarse for recent transmission inference;
- the ecological meaning of a CC depends on whether it is specialist/generalist and on the comparison dataset.

## Exercise 6 — Genome Comparator

Expected conceptual result:
- same-ST isolates need not be genome-wide identical;
- cgMLST reveals within-ST and within-CC structure;
- missing/incomplete loci must be considered when interpreting distances;
- a small allelic distance alone is not enough to claim transmission without temporal, spatial and epidemiological coherence.

---

## Exercise 7 — Microreact

The tree, map and metadata answer different questions:

- **tree:** genomic relatedness;
- **map:** where samples were collected;
- **metadata:** host/source/time/study context.

A spatial overlap does not establish transmission, and phylogenetic proximity does not establish direction.

## AMR discussion

Strong answers distinguish at least three processes:

1. movement of a resistant bacterial lineage;
2. horizontal movement of resistance genes/elements;
3. repeated selection/exposure in separate populations.

Useful follow-up data include:
- long-read/mobile-element context;
- dense temporal sampling;
- matched livestock/human/environmental sampling;
- sewage/water sampling;
- antimicrobial-use/exposure data;
- bird movement/ecological data.
